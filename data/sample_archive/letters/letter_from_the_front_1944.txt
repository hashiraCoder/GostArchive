Field Post Office — No. 74
19 September 1944

Dear Mother and Father,

I hope this finds you both in good health. I am well, though tired, and the mud here would make the lanes at home seem like cobblestone by comparison.

I am not permitted to say where we are exactly. I can tell you it is cold in the mornings now and that the countryside is very flat and cut by many small canals. The unit has been together for eight months and I think we know each other better now than we know ourselves.

My friend Corporal Raymond Elsworth from Coventry has been with me since training. He is a steady man and very good at cards, which matters more than you might think for keeping a fellow's spirits up during a long wait. Raymond's family runs a bakery on Foleshill Road. He says he will go back to that after the war and that there will always be bread for me if I visit.

The worst days are not always the ones with the most noise. Sometimes the quiet is harder to bear because you spend it thinking. I have started keeping these letters in my mind before I write them, composing them while I wait, and I find it helps to have something to say in advance rather than staring at the paper.

I have received your parcels — all three now arrived safely, including the tin of biscuits which I shared with the section. The socks are very welcome. I had not realized how much one thinks about socks until they matter.

I would like, when I come home, to walk across the fields behind the farm before I do anything else. Just that. I think about it some mornings when things are quiet and the light is a certain way.

Tell Aunt Vera I got her letter. I will write to her separately when I have more time.

With all my love,
Private Thomas Ainsworth
No. 7 Platoon, B Company
