Letter to Miss Eliza Cartwright
Written from Thornfield House, Derbyshire
14th of March, 1887

Dear Eliza,

I write to you from the drawing room at Thornfield, where the fire has at last driven the damp from the walls. It has been a winter of considerable hardship, though I trust you have fared better in Hampshire.

You asked after my father's affairs. Father settled the matter of the Hartley estate last autumn, having reached an agreement with Mr. Hartley's solicitors in October. The sum agreed was four hundred and twelve pounds, which Father considered a fair resolution given the disputed boundary along the eastern field. He tells me he is satisfied, though Mother remains unconvinced that any resolution involving the Hartley family can be called fair.

I have taken up the study of botany once more, following Dr. Ashworth's recommendation. He tells me that the moorland specimens I collected last summer are of genuine interest, particularly the small purple flowering plant found near Greywing Pond. He has written to the Royal Botanical Society about it, though I am told such letters rarely receive prompt replies.

The village school opened its new wing on the first of February. Miss Prentice continues as the schoolmistress and tells me the children are learning their letters at a most encouraging pace. I attended the opening with Mother and found the new classroom to be well lit and properly ventilated, which Dr. Ashworth always insisted is the first requirement of good learning.

My brother Thomas returned from Edinburgh last month, having completed his studies in law. He intends to take a position with a firm in Manchester, though he has not yet fixed upon which one. Mother is disappointed he did not choose to remain in Derbyshire, but Thomas has always had his own ideas about where he should plant himself.

Please write when you are able. I find your letters a great comfort during the long winter evenings.

Your affectionate friend,
Margaret Ainsworth
