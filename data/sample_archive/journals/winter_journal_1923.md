# Winter Journal — 1923

## November 8th

First frost last night. I found it on the grass when I went out at seven, white and perfectly even as though someone had gone over it with a paintbrush. By nine it was gone.

I received a reply at last from the County Records Office in Northbridge. The clerk, a Mr. Fentiman, has confirmed that the original deed of 1874 does include the lower meadow in the description of the property. He enclosed a photographic copy of the relevant page. The handwriting is difficult but I can make out the key phrase: "the meadow below the ford, bounded to the south by the Cray Brook." This settles the matter, or should.

I have written to the solicitors about it. Whether they will agree it settles the matter remains to be seen.

## November 17th

Heavy snow. The lane was impassable most of the day. I made bread and read and was content enough.

Agnes telephoned — the exchange in the village has been unreliable this winter but the line was clear for once. She is well and says her roses are cut back and mulched for winter. She asked after the Records Office business and I told her it seemed to be resolved. She said "Good" in the tone that means she had always thought I should have sorted it out sooner, which may be true.

## November 29th

The light goes so early now. I lit the lamps at half past three today and felt the house settle around me in the way it does when there is no one coming.

I have been sorting the papers in Father's old study. There are letters going back to the 1860s, including a correspondence with someone named Harrington in Bristol that I cannot fully make out. It concerns a business arrangement of some kind. I cannot tell whether it came to anything. I have set those letters aside rather than burning them, though I am not sure what I will do with them in the end.

## December 20th

The plaster in the kitchen is definitely worse. I have asked Mr. Barker from the village to look at it in January.

Agnes is coming for Christmas. I have put the spare room in order and found an extra blanket in the cedar chest. I am glad she is coming.
