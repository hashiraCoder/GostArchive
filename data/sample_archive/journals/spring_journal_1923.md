# Spring Journal — 1923

## April 3rd

The thaw came sooner than anyone expected this year. I walked out along the river path after breakfast and found the willows already putting out their pale green. There is something almost alarming about spring when it arrives before you are ready for it.

I have been thinking about the conversation I had with old Mr. Drummond at the cooperative last Thursday. He told me that his grandfather had planted the stand of oaks at the north end of the property in 1851, the year of the Great Exhibition, as a sort of marking of the time. Drummond said the old man wanted something that would outlast a man's memory. I found that a touching idea and have been turning it over since.

## April 11th

Rain for four days without stopping. I spent the mornings working on the account books and the afternoons reading. I have started Middlemarch again, for the third time, and find I understand it differently now that I am no longer young.

My sister Agnes came from York on the Saturday train and stayed through Tuesday. She looks well and has taken up gardening at her new house. She brought me cuttings from a climbing rose she got from a nursery in Harrogate, and I have put them in pots to see if they take.

Agnes asked whether I intended to stay on at the house alone or whether I would consider moving closer to York. I told her I had no immediate plans. She accepted this but I noticed she glanced at the kitchen ceiling where the plaster is coming away near the chimney breast, and said nothing more about it.

## April 22nd

The rose cuttings are showing small white roots when I tipped one out of its pot to check. I put it back quickly and decided to leave the others alone until May.

I have written to the County Records Office in Northbridge about the deed to the lower meadow, which has been in dispute since Father's time. They have not yet replied. I expect I will need to travel there in person, which I am not looking forward to, but the matter has dragged on long enough.

## May 1st

First warm day. Sat outside with tea in the afternoon. The oaks at the north end are coming into leaf — Mr. Drummond's point made visible again.
