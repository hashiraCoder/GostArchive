Notes on the Harrington Correspondence
Compiled from papers found in Father's study, winter 1923

These papers concern a commercial arrangement between a member of the Ainsworth family (identity unclear from the letters — possibly my grandfather, possibly an uncle) and one George Harrington, trading as G. Harrington & Sons, Bristol, manufacturers of waterproof canvas goods.

The correspondence spans approximately 1863 to 1869 and consists of eleven letters in good legible hand. The subject is a supply arrangement for treated canvas panels used in the construction of farm outbuildings. The Ainsworth party appears to have been purchasing these panels in quantity and also, in the later letters, acting as an agent for resale to neighbouring farms.

The correspondence does not explain how the arrangement ended or whether it was profitable. The last letter, dated February 1869, mentions a dispute about a damaged consignment and asks for a credit note, but there is no reply preserved among these papers.

I am not sure who originally received and kept these letters. They were stored in a biscuit tin at the back of the lower drawer of the mahogany desk. The tin is labelled "Harrington/Bristol" in what may be my grandfather's hand.

Whether these records have any practical relevance now, seventy years later, I cannot say. I have kept them for now on the principle that a record once destroyed cannot be recovered.
